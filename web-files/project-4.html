University capstone / 2.5D action-adventure game

Lord Quacksalot and the Soup of Doom

A 2.5D hybrid action-adventure game that blends pixel-art characters with stylized 3D worlds, made by a cross-functional student team.

Project type
University capstone project
My role
Lead character and environmental artist, UI designer
Timeline
1 year (two semesters, 2025)
Tools
Unreal Engine, Maya, Substance Painter, Aseprite, Photoshop

Summary: Jumping between 2D pixel art and 3D worlds across very different genres can leave players lost. As lead artist and UI designer, I gave the 8 enemy characters one consistent look, built three 3D realms around them and designed a UI that tied the worlds together. Feedback from peers and tutors shaped a polished three-stage boss fight.

The problem

The game hops between genres and between 2D and 3D, and it was also my first time working with pixel art and Unreal Engine.

  • Visual disconnect and thematic whiplash: moving between 2D pixel art and fully 3D environments across fast-changing genres, from wild west to horror, risks a jarring look where players feel lost.
  • A steep learning curve: I had no prior game design theory and had to push through imposter syndrome next to peers with heavy coding backgrounds, so I learned the technical skills on the fly.

The goal

  • Cohesive art direction: build one visual language that bridges 2D pixel-art characters and stylized 3D environments.
  • Agile iteration: start with small prototypes and use early feedback from tutors and peers to check the art direction and mechanics before full implementation.
  • Cross-functional collaboration: lean into my strengths as an artist and designer to work with the developers instead of competing with them.

Insights and decisions

Characters must stay readable as the world changes

Finding: the game has three very different realms: a gritty, low-saturation Wild West, a high-contrast black-and-white 1920s Broadway theatre, and an industrial set lit by bright white, red and green sensor lights. With lighting and genre changing so fast, players could easily lose flat 2D sprites against detailed 3D backgrounds.
Decision: I standardized the color palettes and silhouettes of the 8 enemy characters in Photoshop and Aseprite. With one consistent 2D look, they stayed instantly readable in a desaturated desert or under bright industrial spotlights built in Substance Painter.

The UI needs to ground the player

Finding: the dimension-hopping story was intentionally chaotic, so players needed a visual anchor to avoid spatial confusion.
Decision: I designed the core UI, including health bars, menus and loading screens, in one consistent, stylized language that ties the different worlds together.

The solution

  • Themed 2.5D environments: I modeled and textured three distinct 3D realms in Substance Painter, built to frame the 2D characters. The geometry and lighting of each theme communicate the genre shift and its hazards without text tutorials, an approach based on visual affordances and environmental storytelling.
  • Animated trophies: I created and animated 3 unique trophy objects for each level as clear visual goals. These rewards encourage exploration and show players they have mastered a realm’s mechanics, a feedback loop borrowed from gamification.

The results

8enemy characters with one consistent look
3distinct 3D realms
3stages in the polished boss fight

Regular feedback from peers and tutors helped me solve design problems quickly and check my ideas. Adapting to it led to a polished three-stage boss fight where players naturally used everything they had learned. Playtests showed positive player engagement, and the art and development sides worked well together.

What I learned

  • Game design principles like spatial landscaping and character animation transfer well to traditional UX/UI and mobile app design.
  • I overcame imposter syndrome by leaning into my creative strengths and teaching myself outside of class, reaching professional proficiency in Maya, Substance Painter and Unreal Engine.
  • A year-long project vastly improved my presentation skills, so I can explain my creative process clearly to different audiences.
