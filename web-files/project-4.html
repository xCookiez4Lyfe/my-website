Academic project

Lord Quacksalot and the Soup of Doom

A 2.5D hybrid action-adventure game that blends pixel-art characters with stylized 3D worlds, made by a team of 5 students from Monash University.

Project type
Digital game
My role
Lead artist
Timeline
1 year
Tools
UE5, Substance Painter, Aseprite

Summary: A team of 5 students created an action-adventure game by combining 2D characters inside a 3D environment. As lead artist, I designed 8 unique enemy characters, textured environments for 3 different levels and designed UI elements that helped tie them together.

The problem

  • Moving between 2D pixel art and fully 3D environments across different genres risks creating a visual disconnect and thematic whiplash, causing players to feel lost.
  • I had no prior game design theory or experience working on pixel art, so I learned the technical skills on the fly.

The goal

  • Create a cohesive art direction that bridges 2D pixel-art characters and stylized 3D environments.
  • Start with small prototypes and seek early feedback from tutors and peers to constantly improve the art direction and mechanics before full implementation.

Insights & decisions

Characters must stay visible when environments change

Finding: With lighting and textures changing constantly, players could easily lose flat 2D sprites against detailed 3D backgrounds.
Decision: I standardised the color palettes and silhouettes of the 8 enemy characters in order to remain clear in different environments even with various outfits and features.

User interfaces (UI) help track player progress

Finding: The dimension-hopping story was intentionally chaotic, so players needed a visual anchor to avoid confusion.
Decision: I designed UI elements, including health bars, buttons and loading screens, in a consistent style that ties the different levels and characters together.

The solution

  • Themed environments: I textured three distinct 3D levels using different color palettes and materials to indicate the genre shifts without relying on text.
  • Animated trophies: I created and animated trophy objects for each level as a progress indicator. These rewards show players they have mastered a realm’s mechanics.

The results

8enemy character designs
36unique character animations
3themed levels with trophies

Constructive feedback from peers and tutors during playtest sessions helped me solve design problems and keep my ideas grounded. Playtests showed positive player engagement, and they naturally used the mechanics they had learned from different levels in the three-stage boss fight.

What I learned

  • Game design principles like environment and HUD design are transferrable to UX/UI projects.
  • Building testable prototypes early and leaning into my visual design strengths to support the programmers taught me how agile collaboration works in practice.
  • Ensuring characters and HUD elements stayed visible across different environments directly translates to designing accessible UI systems across light and dark modes.
