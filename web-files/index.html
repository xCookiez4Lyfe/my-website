MATT Official Website Redesign

Coming into the new academic year, our goal was to redesign the team's official website to reflect MATT's updated brand identity and improve user experience. The project team consisted of 3 members from the Branding and Design sub-team.

01: Overview

The Problem

The legacy website was functional, but it did not reflect the team's new brand identity - which was more playful and creative. It looked a bit generic, leaned on stock-style graphics, and had clunky, text-heavy menus and odd colour combinations that sometimes hindered readability. Our redesign project was all about giving the website a fresh look, bringing in hand‑drawn assets, improving website navigation, fixing contrast issues, and making our projects and recruitment info feel more engaging in order to boost outreach and user engagement on the team's website.

Add a screenshot or diagram here — e.g. before/after, user flow, or problem framing

02: Research & Process

The Approach

To guide the direction of our redesign project, we conducted a competitive analysis of other student team websites to understand what made them feel engaging, and professional at the same time. Additionally, we conducted an accessibility audit on the legacy site to identify issues such as dense dropdown menus, weak colour contrast, and a static projects section that did not invite exploration. We also gathered input from internal stakeholders by asking team members what they wanted to see on the site, with common requests including more interactivity and animated elements on the website.

These findings were translated into low‑fidelity wireframes and prototypes in Figma, exploring alternative layouts, navigation patterns, and a refined colour palette. After 2–3 rounds of iteration and feedback from the team lead, the selected design direction was approved and then implemented on Squarespace by progressively replacing the legacy pages with the new components and visuals.

Add wireframes, affinity maps, journey maps, or research notes here

03: Final Design

The Outcome

The final website introduces a refreshed homepage, a more interactive projects experience, and clearer recruitment flows, all wrapped in a new visual style built around hand-drawn illustrations and an accessible colour palette. Website navigation is simpler, the projects page now uses a carousel to encourage exploration, and a dedicated Recruitment page guides prospective members through the application process with clear calls to action and expectations. Together, these changes align the site with the team’s new brand identity and provide better support for recruitment and outreach goals.

Designing an Interactive Card Game

A collaboration between Monash Assistive Tech Team (MATT) and the Monash Faculty of IT. The Assistive Futures Workshop centres on an interactive card game that helps participants imagine and design future assistive technologies through playful, guided prompts. Designed for Year 10-12 students and above, it uses a flexible format that can easily adapt to older audiences as well.

01: Overview

The Goal

The card game was designed to provide facilitators witheverything they need to run a smooth, engaging workshop where participants work in teams to tackle real-world accessibility challenges and design imaginative future technologies. With playful visuals and collaborative team play, it aims to inspire creativity and design thinking, helping participants learn, experiment, and pitch ideas without feeling like they are being tested.

Add a screenshot of the original interface or a problem framing diagram

02: Research & Process

The Process

As the Lead Artist on this project, my role focused on creating a consistent visual identity for the card game, ensuring that all elements aligned with the workshop's accessible and inclusive theme. I worked on the individual card designs which included a diverse range of characters, and approached the overall art direction with a cartoon style because I wanted it to be approachable for younger audiences.

The final deck consisting of 17 game cards (7 Scenario Cards and 10 Future Tech Cards) took about 2-3 weeks to be completed and printed out. Before the final implementation, we conducted a playtest session to ensure the designs were ready for the Assistive Futures Workshop on the 21st of September 2025.

03: Final Design

The Outcome

Overall, majority of students rated the workshop highly, with an average engagement score of 4/5. Based on their feedback, it helped them think more deeply about accessibility and the kinds of assistive technologies people might need in real life.

From a personal perspective, the most satisfying moment being the Lead Artist was seeing the visual style of the game appeal to the target audience. The positive engagement scores and “fun and engaging” feedback suggests that the card designs and overall visual tone supported the inclusive, low-pressure experience the we were aiming for.
